# NX Port Studio 1.6.0 RC10

This candidate gathers what I have tested of online and local play into one table, and adds a host change for the peer connection that follows Nextendo matchmaking. It also carries RC9's Splatoon 3 12.0.0 graphics and online fixes.

## Games with online and local play

The README now has a [table of the Switch games whose network modes I have tested](../../README.md#games-with-online-and-local-play), with each one's Nextendo, LAN and local wireless (LDN) status:

- **Mario Kart 8 Deluxe:** one complete Worldwide race on 4.0.1. On 3.0.5, a complete LAN race between my Mac and my Switch, and LDN sessions across Mac, iPhone and my Switch.
- **Super Smash Bros. Ultimate 13.0.5:** one arena round against a remote player.
- **Splatoon 2 (Europe) 5.5.2:** the online lobby and Turf War queue.
- **Splatoon 3 12.0.0:** the lobby and matchmaking queue, with the NAT check passed.

The table says which steps I completed and which I only reached, and lists the known limits.

## Nextendo: a UDP socket keeps its router mapping

Pia runs its NAT check on one UDP socket, closes it, and binds a new socket on the same port for the match. On the Switch that socket lives in the firmware. Here each one was a separate Mac socket, and a router that ties its mapping to the socket can give the new one a different public port, so the other player probes a dead endpoint. In Nextendo mode the host now holds a closed UDP socket for 5 seconds and hands it back when the game binds the same address again ([reference entry](../reference/services.md#a-udp-sockets-router-mapping-across-pias-close-and-rebind)). The game's options on its new socket carry over, and anything received while the socket was held is discarded. LAN, LDN and offline modes are unchanged, and `DM_NET_UDP_KEEP=0` turns this off.

This affects every game that plays through Pia: Smash, Splatoon 2 and Splatoon 3 among them. I have not yet completed a Nextendo match with it, so it does not qualify any of them.

## Leads I checked

- **Smash Quickplay.** The game imports ten `nn::nifm` functions and the host answers all ten; it asks for no connection status or signal quality. Its failed proxy-setting query only produces a log line, and the game continues as with no proxy. Neither explains the unstable-network rejection, which leaves the UDP mapping above as the next thing to test.
- **Splatoon 3 matchmaking.** The provider's published server source forms a match only from players searching the same mode at the same time. It keeps the full count (8 for Turf War, 4 for Salmon Run) for 45 seconds, then lowers it, never below half. With few people online the queue can wait indefinitely, which most likely explains why I was never assigned a match. Salmon Run with a second player is the quickest way to test a match. LAN play starts in The Shoal by holding ZL and ZR and then pressing and holding the Left Stick.

## What I checked

- `t_net` adds the held-socket checks: the same Mac socket after a rebind, carried-over options, discarded datagrams, a working send, a second bind of a bound socket still failing, and a fresh socket for another address, a connected socket, `DM_NET_UDP_KEEP=0` and LAN. All 202 checks pass, also under ASan/UBSan. With keeping turned off, the same-socket check fails.
- The whole host suite and the documentation tests pass.

## Limits

I have not completed a Splatoon 3 online match, nor Smash Quickplay, with this candidate. LAN and LDN remain qualified only for Mario Kart 8 Deluxe 3.0.5, and online play on iPhone is not qualified. Rebuild a port to get the host change; updating Studio does not update an exported app.
